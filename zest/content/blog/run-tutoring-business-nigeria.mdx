Tutoring is booming in Nigeria. From WAEC prep in Lagos to university-level coaching in Abuja, families are investing more in private education every year. But while demand is high, most tutors struggle with the business side — scheduling conflicts, chasing payments, and losing students to disorganised processes. If you want to build a sustainable tutoring business, you need more than subject knowledge. You need a system.

## Set your rates with confidence

One of the first questions new tutors ask is: "What should I charge?" The honest answer is that it depends on your level, the subject, and your location. But here are some realistic ranges to anchor your thinking:

- **Primary school tutoring:** ₦3,000 – ₦5,000 per session
- **Secondary school (JSS1–SS3):** ₦5,000 – ₦10,000 per session
- **WAEC/NECO/UTME prep:** ₦7,000 – ₦12,000 per session
- **University and professional exams:** ₦10,000 – ₦15,000+ per session

You can charge per hour, per session, or offer package pricing. Package pricing — say, 12 sessions for a term — tends to work best because it gives parents cost certainty and gives you guaranteed income. Don't undervalue yourself. If parents trust you enough to bring their children, trust your rates too.

## Create a schedule that works

Juggling multiple students without a system is a recipe for burnout. The tutors who last are the ones who treat their time like a business asset.

Block out your available hours first. Decide whether you tutor mornings, afternoons, or evenings — and stick to it. Build in buffer time between sessions so you're not rushing from one student to the next. Fifteen minutes between lessons is enough to reset and grab water.

If you tutor across multiple locations — say, Lekki and Ikeja — group students by area on the same day. The less you commute, the more time you have for actual teaching.

And please, stop relying on your memory. Use a calendar, a spreadsheet, anything. Forgetting a session or double-booking destroys trust faster than anything else.

## Get more students

The best tutors in Nigeria don't just teach well — they market themselves consistently. Here are the channels that actually work:

- **WhatsApp status:** Post short tips, student testimonials, or exam countdowns. Parents scroll WhatsApp daily. Be visible.
- **Parent referrals:** After every successful exam or term, ask happy parents to recommend you. A single parent in a school WhatsApp group can bring five new students.
- **School partnerships:** Approach schools about being a recommended after-school tutor. Some schools welcome this because it helps their results.
- **Social media:** Short-form video content on Instagram and TikTok works incredibly well for tutors. A 60-second maths trick can go viral in your local area.

## Professional booking flow

Here's where most tutors lose students: the "DM me" approach. When a parent messages you asking about tutoring, and you reply with a wall of text about your availability, pricing, and process — they often just disappear. It feels informal, hard to commit to, and difficult to track.

A better flow looks like this:

1. **Share a booking link.** Parents click, see your available times, and pick a slot. Simple.
2. **Use an intake form.** Ask about the student's current level, subjects, and goals before the first session. This lets you prepare and shows professionalism.
3. **Confirm automatically.** Send a confirmation with date, time, and location (or Zoom link) so there's no back-and-forth.

This isn't complicated to set up, but it transforms how people perceive you. You go from "that tutor I found online" to "this person runs a proper operation."

## Manage payments

Nigerian parents prefer bank transfers. That's fine. The problem isn't the method — it's the tracking. Tutors frequently lose money because they can't remember who paid, who owes, and who was supposed to pay last week.

Here's what works:

- **Keep a payment log.** Every student, every session, every payment. A simple spreadsheet is better than nothing.
- **Send receipts.** Even a short WhatsApp message — "Payment received, thanks! Next session is Thursday" — builds trust and reduces disputes.
- **Follow up on overdue payments early.** Don't wait two weeks. A polite message after 48 hours keeps things professional.

## Start small, build systems

You don't need an office or a team to run a successful tutoring business. You need a clear rate structure, a consistent schedule, a way to attract students, and a professional booking and payment flow. These systems compound over time. The tutor who shows up consistently and communicates professionally will always outperform the one who's brilliant but disorganised.
