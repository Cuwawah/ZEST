Most coaches in Nigeria don't struggle because they're bad at coaching. They struggle because clients can't book them easily. You post on Instagram, drop your number on WhatsApp, tell a few friends — and then nothing happens. The calendar stays empty.

The fix isn't more marketing. It's removing the barriers between "I'm interested" and "I just booked a session."

Here's how to fill your coaching schedule with strategies that work in the Nigerian market.

## Stop relying on word of mouth

Word of mouth is great — when it shows up. But it's unpredictable. One month you get three referrals. The next, zero. You can't build a business on hope.

Referrals alone aren't enough because:

- People forget to refer you, even when they love your work
- They don't always know who in their network needs coaching
- The referral process is slow — by the time someone passes your name along, the potential client may have already booked someone else

If you're starting from scratch, check out our guide on [how to start a coaching business in Nigeria](/blog/start-coaching-business-nigeria) for the foundational steps.

You need a system that brings in clients consistently. That means getting visible and making booking frictionless.

## Make it stupid easy to book

Here's the truth: if a potential client has to DM you, wait for a reply, send their details, wait again, then finally get a time — most of them will drop off. Every extra step costs you clients.

The solution is a booking link.

A booking link lets someone pick a time, confirm, and they're done. No back-and-forth. No "let me check my schedule." No lost messages in WhatsApp.

What a good booking system does:

- Shows your available slots in real time
- Works on mobile (most Nigerians are on their phones)
- Sends automatic reminders so people don't ghost you
- Accepts payment upfront or at booking

Once you have a booking link, put it everywhere — your WhatsApp bio, Instagram stories, Twitter/X profile, even your email signature. Make it impossible to miss.

## Ask for referrals the right way

Referrals still matter. But most coaches ask badly — or don't ask at all.

The right way to ask for referrals:

**Timing matters.** Ask right after a great session, when the client is energised and grateful. Not weeks later. Not in a random WhatsApp message.

**Be specific.** Don't say "if you know anyone who needs coaching, send them my way." Say "Do you have a colleague who's been struggling with [specific problem]? I'd love to help them."

**Make it easy to share.** Send them your booking link along with a short message they can forward. Something like: "Here's a link to book a free intro call with my coach — [link]."

**Follow up.** If someone says they'll refer you, check back in a week. Not to pressure them — just to remind them. People get busy.

## Show up where your clients are

You don't need to be on every platform. You need to be on the ones where your clients actually spend time.

For most Nigerian coaches, that's:

- **WhatsApp.** Update your status regularly. Share quick tips, client wins (with permission), and behind-the-scenes content. Your status is free advertising — use it.
- **LinkedIn.** Especially if you coach professionals or entrepreneurs. Post insights, comment on posts, and connect with potential clients directly.
- **Twitter/X.** Great for building authority. Share threads with practical advice. Engage in conversations and answer questions in your niche publicly.

The goal isn't to go viral. It's to stay visible. When someone in your network thinks "I need a coach," your name should be the first one that comes to mind.

## Follow up with past clients

This is the most overlooked strategy in coaching. Past clients already trust you. Rebooking them takes a fraction of the effort of landing a new one.

Yet most coaches never follow up. They deliver a great session and then disappear until the client remembers to reach out.

How to follow up effectively:

- **Send a check-in message** a week after your last session. Ask how they're implementing what you discussed. Offer a quick tip.
- **Share relevant content.** If you find an article or resource that relates to their goals, send it over. Shows you're thinking about them.
- **Offer a rebooking discount or package.** Something like "If you book 3 sessions this month, the third one is 20% off."
- **Ask for feedback.** It opens the door to a natural conversation about working together again.

Past clients are your warmest leads. Treat them that way.

One underrated barrier to getting coaching clients: payment friction. If you can only accept bank transfers and have to manually share account details every time, you're making the process harder than it needs to be.

The easier it is to pay, the more likely people are to complete the booking.

## The bottom line

Getting more coaching clients in Nigeria isn't about doing more. It's about removing the obstacles that stop interested people from booking you.

Get a booking link. Put it everywhere. Ask for referrals with a clear message. Show up consistently on one or two platforms. Follow up with past clients. And make payment easy.
