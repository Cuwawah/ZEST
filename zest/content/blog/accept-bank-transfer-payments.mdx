If you run a service business in Nigeria, you've probably noticed something: most of your clients prefer to pay by bank transfer.

They don't want to enter card details. They don't trust online payment gateways. They'd rather open their banking app, transfer the money, and send you a screenshot.

The question is: how do you make this process seamless? Whether you're accepting bank transfers or exploring a [Calendly alternative](/blog/calendly-alternative-nigeria) for booking, simplifying your workflow matters.

## Why bank transfers work best in Nigeria

### 1. No card required
Not every client has a debit card. But every client has a bank account. Bank transfers remove the biggest barrier to payment.

### 2. No transaction fees (for the payer)
Card payments often charge the client extra. Bank transfers are free for the sender. That means less friction at checkout.

### 3. Familiar and trusted
Nigerians trust their banks. A bank transfer feels safer than entering card details on an unfamiliar website.

### 4. Instant confirmation
With modern banking apps, transfers show up in seconds. You can confirm payment almost immediately.

## The challenge: matching payments to clients

The problem with bank transfers is manual matching. When 10 people transfer ₦4,000 to your account, how do you know which payment belongs to which client?

This is where most service providers waste hours — scrolling through bank notifications, asking "did you pay?", and manually updating spreadsheets.

## Best practices for bank transfer payments

1. **Display your bank details clearly** — on your website, booking page, and invoices
2. **Include a payment reference** — or use unique amounts (like Zest does)
3. **Send payment confirmation** — a WhatsApp message saying "Payment received, you're all set"
4. **Follow up on unmatched payments** — set a reminder to check daily
5. **Keep records** — track who paid, when, and for what
