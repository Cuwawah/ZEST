Every week, thousands of Nigerian coaches, tutors, therapists, and photographers lose hours to the same conversation:

*"Are you available on Thursday?"*
*"Thursday morning works, what time?"*
*"Can you do 4pm instead?"*
*"Let me check..."*

This happens 20 times a week for a busy service provider. That's 20 times they could have lost a client to someone who replied faster. The problem isn't that scheduling is hard. The problem is that the tools built to fix this were made for a different kind of market.

## Why imported tools don't work here

Calendly, Acuity, Square Appointments — good products. But they were built for places where:

- Clients pay with credit cards
- Communication happens over email
- Most bookings are cold — the client found you online and doesn't know you yet
- The payment is the relationship

That's not how Nigerian service businesses work. Here's the reality:

- **WhatsApp is where business happens.** Not email. Not SMS. WhatsApp. Every conversation with a client starts and ends there.
- **Bank transfer is how people pay.** Most clients don't have credit cards. They open their banking app, send the money, and maybe send you a screenshot. That's it.
- **Repeat clients are the business.** The first booking is the easy part. The real money is in the second, third, tenth booking — the parent who books tutoring every term, the couple who comes back for photos each year, the client who renews coaching every quarter.

Calendly handles scheduling. It doesn't handle the fact that your best client hasn't booked in 6 weeks and you should probably reach out. It doesn't handle the fact that 67% of your clients never come back, and you don't know why. It doesn't handle the fact that your client paid by transfer and you need to figure out who sent that money.

These are different problems. Not missing features — different problems.

## The booking moment vs. the whole relationship

Here's what I keep thinking about: most booking tools care about the **moment** — the 30 seconds where a client picks a time. But the business isn't the moment. It's what comes before and after.

Before: Has this client booked before? What did they tell me last time? What should I ask before we meet?

After: Did they come back? When was the last time? Are they drifting away?

No scheduling tool deals with this. They're all built around the calendar — available slots, booked slots, buffer times. That's fine, but it's the least interesting part of the problem.

## What I mean by "client memory"

I've been thinking about this as **client memory** — the idea that your booking system should know who your clients are, not just when they're free.

A scheduling tool remembers time. Client memory remembers:

- That Adaeze has booked 7 times and is your most loyal client
- That Chukwuemeka hasn't booked in 45 days and you should reach out
- That every parent who asks about JAMB prep also asks about pricing first
- That your busiest month is September and you should open more slots
- That 3 of your last 5 new clients never came back — maybe something in your process is pushing them away

This is what actually grows a service business. Not a prettier calendar. Not another notification. Knowing who matters and when to do something about it.

## What I'm building

I started building Zest because I couldn't find a tool that worked like Nigerian service businesses actually work. Not another Calendly with a Nigerian flag on it — something built from the ground up for bank transfers, WhatsApp, and repeat clients.

It's a booking platform, but the useful part isn't the scheduling. It's that it tracks who comes back, who's drifting, and what changed between visits. It includes intake forms on every plan — so you know what a client needs before they arrive. It handles bank transfers and activates your account automatically. It works with WhatsApp because that's where your clients already are.

I'm not trying to build Calendly for Africa. I'm trying to build the tool I wished existed when I watched service providers in Lagos lose clients because a WhatsApp reply took too long.

## What this isn't

I'll be honest: this isn't for everyone. If you run a high-volume business — a salon chain with 50 stylists and walk-in clients — Zest isn't for you. It's for the individual provider or small practice doing 10 to 50 bookings a month, where each client matters and losing one person to a slow reply actually hurts.

And the client tracking is still early. It watches patterns and tells you what's happening, but it's not a full CRM. It won't send automated marketing emails or build sales funnels. It does one thing: helps you understand your clients so you can serve them better. If that's what you need, it works. If you need a do-everything tool, there are better options.

## The bigger picture

I think there's a whole category of software that was brought into African markets without being rebuilt for how those markets actually work. Booking tools are one example. Invoicing, client management, payments — there's a long list of tools that work great in San Francisco and fall apart in Lagos. Not because the engineering is bad, but because the assumptions are wrong.

The assumption that clients pay with cards. The assumption that email is the main channel. The assumption that every booking is a cold transaction. The assumption that the job is done once the slot is booked.

I don't think the answer is "build Calendly but African." I think the answer is start from the relationship — the WhatsApp thread, the bank transfer, the client who's been with you for two years — and build from there.

That's what I'm trying to do with Zest. Not a scheduling tool. A way to remember your clients.
